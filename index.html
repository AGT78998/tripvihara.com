Rome shaped Europe,
Bihar shaped Asia.

Every November, millions gather along Bihar's rivers to celebrate Chhath, one of world's oldest living traditions of sun worship. Yet this living tradition is only one chapter in a much longer story.

From the ideas of governance found in Chanakya's Arthashastra to the Jataka tales, one of world's oldest collections of animal fables, sharing striking parallels with Aesop's Fables, half a world away—

Bihar was not on the margins of ancient history—it was one of its greatest centers of thought, learning, and governance.

This is not myth or memory. It is history that you can walk through today.

Chapter I

The Greatest Classroom of the Ancient World

Imagine a university where monks debated philosophy, astronomers studied the stars, physicians learned medicine, and travelers crossed mountains simply to study. For centuries, Nalanda was one of the world's greatest centres of learning. Today, its quiet ruins still carry the echoes of those conversations.

History Never Really Left

Empires rose. Great universities flourished. Ideas changed the world. Through every chapter of Bihar's story, the Ganga kept flowing—connecting people, cultures and generations. Even today, its banks remain part of everyday life, reminding visitors that history here isn't locked behind museum glass. It continues to breathe.

Cut From Rock, Polished to a Mirror

More than 2,200 years ago, these granite chambers were carved into solid rock and polished to a mirror-like finish that still astonishes visitors today. And that's not even its most interesting feature. Built during the Mauryan Empire, the Barabar Caves are one of world's oldest surviving rock-cut caves.

Some places preserve history. Bihar still lives it.
